# How the Zap AI works

No language model plays any move. Zap is a small neural network trained by self-play, with rule-based players as baselines and as the fallback.

## Players

| Player | What it is | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random | picks any legal action | `ai/src/agent.ts` |
| Heuristic | hand estimate from the cards and rounds, thresholds for bets, wins rounds as cheaply as possible | `ai/src/heuristic.ts` |
| Tuned heuristic | the same code with parameters found by search in duplicate matches | `ai/bench/tune-heuristic.ts`, `ai/models/heuristic-tuned.json` |
| Zap | the network, trained by imitation of the heuristic and then by regularized PPO in self-play | `ai/src/net-agent.ts`, `training/` |
| Characters | the same network with knobs applied at decision time | `ai/src/personas.ts` |

Every player receives only an `Observation` (`engine/src/observe.ts`): the same view a person has at that seat. Since milestone 6 a player may also receive an `AgentContext` (`ai/src/agent.ts`): the public history of the match so far (`engine/src/public.ts`), the same for every seat. Nothing else reaches the decision code, so an AI cannot see hidden cards, and the tests check that observations and the public history never contain them.

## Why this approach

Truco has hidden cards, bluffing, bets that can be accepted or refused, partners, and a score that changes the value of each decision. The options compared before building:

* CFR and Monte Carlo CFR give equilibrium guarantees for two players, not for teams, and need weeks of computation with abstractions.
* Deep CFR and similar methods retrain networks every iteration; expensive on a CPU-only server.
* Search at decision time (information set Monte Carlo tree search, ReBeL) does not fit in 10 ms of CPU per request on the Workers free plan, and plain sampling search cannot value hiding information.
* DouZero-style Monte Carlo learning works well for teams but picks deterministic bets, which are easy to exploit.
* PPO with a regularization towards a slowly moving anchor (magnetic mirror descent) matched or beat those methods in recent work on imperfect-information games (Rudolph et al., ICLR 2026), and PPO with a critic that sees all cards during training beat DouZero in DouDizhu (PerfectDou). The policy samples its actions, so bluffs come in measured doses.

No published 2 against 2 truco AI with measured strength was found. The closest work is 1 against 1: a Deep CFR bot for Argentine truco (tomzvilla/truco-cfr), CFR for Uruguayan truco, and case-based reasoning for gaucho truco.

## Network

* Inputs (features v1): 431 numbers from the seat's view, from its own point of view: room options, score, hand state, bets, position, own cards sorted by strength, the partner's cards when the mão de onze shows them, the cards played this round and in earlier rounds, cards already seen by strength class, signals seen, cards left per seat, and a character vector. Cards are described by strength class (0 to 13), since suits matter only for manilhas. See `ai/src/features.ts`.
* Features v2 add 30 numbers: public statistics of the other players in this match (see "Reading the opponent during a match" below), 461 inputs in all. The model file says which layout it uses (`features` in the header: `v1`, `v2` or `v2-empty`), and every consumer builds the inputs with `encoderFor(header)`, so v1 files keep working everywhere.
* Outputs: 15 action scores (play or cover the strongest, middle or weakest card; raise; accept; run; six signals), illegal ones masked, and the chance of winning the match.
* Shape: 431 inputs, two hidden layers of 256 with ReLU, about 180 thousand weights. Online it uses 8-bit weights with one scale per row (about 180 KB).
* The forward pass is written once in TypeScript (`ai/src/mlp.ts`) and used by the self-play workers, the promotion gate and the Durable Object. `training/tests/check_model_format.py` checks that the learner's files give the same outputs there.

## Training

* Workers (`training/src/selfplay.ts`) play full matches with the real rules engine and write one record per decision of the learning seats: quantized inputs, the hidden cards of the other seats (for the critic only), the legal actions, the action, its probability and the reward.
* Each hand is an episode per seat. Its reward is the change in the team's chance of winning the match, computed by dynamic programming over the score (`ai/src/winprob.ts`). Partners share the reward, so the AI plays for the team.
* The learner (`training/learner.py`, PyTorch on CPU) first imitates the heuristic, then runs PPO with an entropy bonus and a penalty for moving away from an anchor policy that is refreshed every 20 iterations. A separate critic sees every card and lowers the variance of the advantages; it never leaves training.
* Opponents (milestone 6 league, `ai/src/league.ts`): every member of the evaluation panel takes part, since a network trained only against one heuristic lost to another. Shares per match: the current network 30% (both teams recorded), its last 8 snapshots 10%, v1 Zap 8%, the tuned heuristic 5%, the base heuristic 5%, random 2%, the five characters (Otávio, Bento, Lia, Ravi and Iara) 16%, extreme styles 14% and players that switch style during the match 10%. In 2 against 2 the second opponent is the same player half of the time, and one partner of the learning team is sometimes replaced, so the network does not depend on a partner identical to itself. The training cycle adds two things: the last 3 promoted versions stay in the league for the whole run as fixed files (`train.py --league-fixed`), and, once the human behaviour model passed its test, 8% of the matches move from the current network to that model (kind `human`, `train.py --human-model`), as opponent and as partner. Both are absent from the runs of milestone 6.
* Extreme styles (`ai/src/styles.ts`) play the cards with v1 and make every bet decision by a fixed rule: raises whenever it may, never raises, never runs, always runs, bluffs with weak hands, covers often (about 45% of the plays where covering is allowed), and mixtures of two of them. A switching player uses one style for 1 to 6 hands and then another.
* Blind matches: in 15% of the matches (`train.py --no-stats-share`) the learning seats read no statistics for the whole match. Otherwise the policy with no evidence would only ever be trained in the first hand, and it is what the network plays without evidence, while adaptation is switched off, and at a table with a person who opted out.
* Each worker draws the setup of a match (rules, learning team, opponents) from one random stream and every action from another, so two runs with the same seeds meet the same setups and deals.
* Room options are sampled during training and are inputs of the network.

## Evaluation

* Duplicate format: every deal is played twice with the teams swapped, and a pair scores 1, 0.5 or 0. Luck from the cards cancels out.
* Reported per variant (paulista, mineiro) and table size (1 against 1, 2 against 2), with 95% confidence intervals over pairs.
* The network only goes live if it beats the tuned heuristic by at least 2 percentage points in this format. Otherwise the tuned heuristic plays, and the site says so.

## Characters

All characters use the same network. Each one changes the action scores before sampling: temperature, offsets for raising, accepting and running, and an extra raise score with a weak hand (bluffs). Ravi switches style from hand to hand. The offsets are calibrated for each model version so the style shows in the numbers (`ai/bench/calibrate-personas.ts`), and the profile cards show statistics measured in self-play, not the knob values.

The calibration, for each version (the training cycle runs it on every candidate, with the gate's statistics mode and threads):

1. Style shares by bisection: Otávio asks for truco half as often as Zap, Bento bluffs 20 points more than Zap, Lia asks for truco 30 points more. A share is the part of the bet decisions that follow the character's betting style; card play always comes from the network.
2. All six are measured as a team against a team of Zap on the same model, 2 times `pairs` duplicate pairs per segment (the cycle: 200, so 400 per segment, 1,600 pooled).
3. The difficulty order must hold with a margin of 3 points: Otávio at least 3 points below the easiest of Bento, Lia and Ravi, and Iara at least 3 points above the hardest of them. While it does not, one change per side and round, and the changed characters are measured again on the same deals: Ravi, when he is the easiest of the middle, follows his styles in fewer bet decisions (share down 0.15, not below 0.4); otherwise Otávio's temperature goes up 0.3, at most 2.4; Iara's share goes down 0.1, then her temperature.
4. Separation, where cheap: Iara's share is tried at 0.45, 0.55 and 0.65, and the largest that keeps her twice the margin above the middle and tells her apart from Zap at least 5 points better is kept. Twice the margin, because the choice is judged on the deals it was made on: for v2, a share chosen 3.8 points above the middle measured 0.7 points above it on the gate's fresh deals, so that first calibration was dropped and run again with this rule.

The gate then measures the six again on fresh deals of the cycle's seed (`training/cycle/gate/personas-order.ts`) and fails a candidate whose order is reversed beyond noise. Measurement runs on worker threads (`ai/bench/persona-measure.ts`); the numbers do not depend on how many.

## Reading the opponent during a match

The adaptive network (features v2) reads how the other players have behaved so far in the current match and adjusts its play. It learned this by itself in training, against players of very different styles. No rule tells it what to do with what it reads.

### What is counted

Only public facts of finished hands of the current match count (`ai/src/opponent-stats.ts`): what everyone at the table saw. Cards that went back to the deck unseen and cards played face down never count, for anyone. Nothing is kept from one match to the next. For each other seat (the next, the partner, the previous):

| Number | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| raise | share of normal hands in which the player asked for truco or raised |
| run, accept, reraise | shares of the bets the player faced that were refused, accepted or raised again |
| shown | mean strength of the cards the player showed face up in hands where they raised (0 weakest, 1 the zap) |
| cover | share of the plays where covering was allowed that were made face down |
| four counts | how many hands, answers, cards shown and coverable plays stand behind each share |

Every share is pulled towards a population value (the priors) with the weight of 2 observations, so a single hand never reads as a strong habit. The priors were measured in self-play of v1 against the training league and travel inside each model file (`meta.priors`), so measuring them again later never changes an old model. The network also sees the counts, on a scale that grows like a logarithm, so it can tell a habit seen in 20 hands from one seen in 2. Mão de onze decisions are not counted.

### Starting small: the regularizer and the safety net

Adjustments must start small and grow with the evidence. The evidence n of a decision is how many betting events stand behind it: hands in which the opponent asked for truco plus raises they answered (the raiser when answering a raise, otherwise the least known opponent).

* In training, a penalty keeps the policy that reads the statistics close to the same network with the statistics at their no evidence value, with a weight nW / (nW + n) that halves at typical mid-match evidence (`training/learner.py`).
* At decision time (`ai/src/safety.ts`), the move of the policy away from the no evidence policy is capped by a budget of KL divergence B(n) = Bmax n / (n + nHalf): nothing may move without evidence, and the budget grows towards Bmax as evidence builds up. When the network wants to move further, the two distributions are mixed geometrically with the largest weight that fits the budget. The budget bounds what each character actually plays: the character's offsets and temperature are applied to both distributions first, so the KL between what the character plays and what the same character would play without evidence never exceeds B(n). Bmax and nHalf are chosen by a sweep and written into the model file (`meta.adapt`); an exported v2 model without them is refused (`ai/bench/export-model.ts`).
* The safety sweep (`ai/bench/adaptation-sweep.ts --mode safety`) works in two stages: every setting on a first set of deals, then the three most promising settings whose proxy explorers did not gain on average, replayed on new deals with six times as many pairs. The final setting is the one with the best gain against the styles among those whose proxy explorer gain stays under 0.5 point (one-sided 95% bound). The explorer test can tighten it afterwards (runbook step 7): the sweep chose Bmax 0.5, nHalf 1 (`ai.adaptation.safetyNet`), and after attempt 2 of the explorer test failed, the export was written again with Bmax 0.2, nHalf 1 (SHA-256 fa30e9a5). That file failed too, and the network was then fine-tuned against opponents that fake a pattern (the feints league, "Training the adaptive network" below). Its export with the same safety net, `training/runs/m6-adaptive-r/export/zap-v2-b0.2-h1.zapw` (SHA-256 7223c227), is the candidate that passed. Every key about the candidate's own play now measures that file: `perStyle`, `ablation`, `headToHead`, `decisionTime`, `personasV2`, `readingCurve`, the explorer verdicts and `switchOnCheck` (the local run of the owner's path to switch the reading on, `DEPLOY.md` section 9). The sweeps measured their own runs. `ai.adaptation.history` keeps, per earlier file, the keys that measured it (fa30e9a5, and 45c541c5 for the file with Bmax 0.5, nHalf 1), and `explorer.criterion.history` lists the verdicts against earlier files.

### The twin and the explorer

* The twin (features `v2-empty`) is trained with the same seeds, warm start, league and compute, with the statistics always at no evidence. The difference between the adaptive network and the twin is what training with the statistics adds, mixed with how far two training runs land apart by chance; a second twin trained with other seeds measures that distance once. The candidate that passed has its own twin, `training/runs/m6-twin-r`, fine-tuned exactly like it; the second twin stopped at iteration 390, so for that candidate the second twin minus the twin mixes the seeds with the extra training and is not a seed floor (`ai.adaptation.perStyle.seedCheck`; the seed floor of two twins with the same training is in `ai.adaptation.history`). The same network with the statistics forced empty (ablation) measures what reading adds without that chance, and it is also what the room plays while adaptation is off and at a table with a person who opted out. Runbook step 10 serves the adaptive file while adaptation is off only when that network is not worse than the twin; otherwise the twin is served while adaptation is off.
* Measurement (a) plays the training styles and styles that never sit in a training match. Since the feints fine-tune these are two: `coinFlip`, which asks for truco or raises at random whatever its cards, so its raises say nothing about its hand, and `coversAll`, which plays face down in about three plays out of four where covering is allowed. `trapper` (never asks for truco first, never runs, raises back with a fair hand) was held out until that fine-tune made it a phase of the feints. The held out styles are far from every training style in the numbers the network reads.
* Every opponent of a measurement plays the same deals, so pooled intervals count each deal once across opponents (`pooledPairedSummary` in `ai/src/evaluate.ts`).
* The explorer test looks for an opponent that fakes a pattern and then switches, to exploit the reading. Two kinds are measured.
  * Scripted explorers (`ai/bench/explorer.ts`): the search covers 1,008 such players: eight styles, a switch at a hand from 1 to 8 or once the explorer has shown 2, 4 or 8 betting events, and two partner options. Before the feints fine-tune no training opponent switched that way; the feints league of the current candidate switches at a hand from 1 to 8 or after showing from 2 to 8 betting events (`ai.adaptation.explorer.trainingFamily.feints`). It runs on one set of deals; the best one, and the best one from outside the switching players met in training, are then measured on fresh deals against the adaptive network, the twin and the ablated network. Each attempt uses deals no earlier attempt played, in the search and in the fresh measurement. Attempt 1 ranked the candidates by the gain over the twin, so it looked for explorers of the base part; attempt 2, with the same safety net, ranked them by the adaptation part (`--baseline ablated`), on selection deals after those of attempt 1 (seed e5 from pair 1,360 per segment) and fresh deals after those of attempt 1 (seed f7, pairs 2,000 to 3,999 per segment). Attempt 3 searched again, ranked by the adaptation part, against the file exported with the tighter safety net Bmax 0.2, nHalf 1 (seed e5 from pair 2,720, fresh pairs 4,000 to 5,999). Attempt 4 is no search: it measured the explorer of attempt 2 again against that file (`--remeasure`, fresh pairs 6,000 to 7,999), because the criterion judges every explorer against the file that would be served. Attempts 5 to 8 judged the fine-tuned candidate (SHA-256 7223c227), each on fresh deals no earlier attempt played (seed f7, from pair 8,000, 10,000, 12,000 and 14,000 per segment). Attempt 5 is a new search ranked by the adaptation part, over the original grid and a fresh family of shapes no training league draws: three phases, back and forth every hand, the held out styles, and a partner on its own schedule. Attempts 6, 7 and 8 measured again the explorers of attempts 1, 2 and 3 (`ai.adaptation.explorer.attempts`, each with `remeasureOf`).
  * A trained explorer (`ai/bench/explorer-trained.ts`): a network trained with PPO as a best response against the frozen adaptive network as the room serves it (statistics live, its safety net, Zap knobs), which holds the other team. It starts from v2, the twin of the first run (`training/runs/m6-twin`), not from the candidate's own twin `training/runs/m6-twin-r` (`training/explorer_init.py` moves that file's weights on the statistics into the bias, so it starts by playing as v2; `ai.adaptation.explorer.trained.training.startFrom`). It reads its own public statistics, the next seat's and its partner's (features `v2-self`: what the adaptive network reads about it, and through the count of hands how far the match has gone). It learns with one episode per match, so what it shows in early hands is credited with what it earns later (`training/train.py --features v2-self --league-as-served --episode match --eval-served`). It is measured on fresh deals against the adaptive network, the ablated network, the twin and the second twin, and its public statistics early and late in the match say what it learned to do. The explorer of the current candidate (`training/runs/m6-explorer-r`) trained against that file itself (SHA-256 7223c227, safety net Bmax 0.2, nHalf 1) for all 103 of its planned iterations, 98 of them PPO, in 76.4 minutes: in the periodic checks its score against the adaptive network went from 46.7% at the start to 59.3% at iteration 100, so a longer run might find more (`ai.adaptation.explorer.trained.training`). ### Training the adaptive network

* Warm start (`training/warm_start.py`): v1's weights, with zero weights on the 30 new inputs, so at the start the adaptive network plays exactly as v1 (checked on 400 matches by `training/tests/check_warm_start.py`). The critic is trained alone for 10 iterations before PPO starts.
* `training/train.py --features v2 --priors ai/models/opponent-priors.json --init-actor ... --adapt-beta B --adapt-nw NW` for the adaptive run, and the same line with `--features v2-empty` for the twin.
* The feints fine-tune: after the first file with Bmax 0.2, nHalf 1 failed the explorer test, both networks went on from their iteration 390 to iteration 590 (`--resume-from`, runs `training/runs/m6-adaptive-r` and `training/runs/m6-twin-r`, with the same seeds) with a league that adds the feints kind: training opponents that play one style and switch to another at a hand or after showing some evidence, `trapper` among their phases (`ai/src/league.ts`, `ai/src/styles.ts`). The fine-tune took 1.735 hours for the adaptive network and 1.742 hours for the twin (`ai.adaptation.training.runs`, which counts the iterations of each run folder). The candidate that passed is iteration 590 of the adaptive run, exported with Bmax 0.2, nHalf 1; v2, in play, is iteration 390 of the first twin run.

### In the room

* The room gives every AI move the public history of the match (`worker/src/room-core.ts`). When a person at the table switched on "Don't adapt to me", the AIs read no evidence about anyone at that table for the whole match: the network plays its no evidence policy, which blind training matches keep trained, instead of a mix of empty and filled statistics it never saw. The mask is fixed when a match starts and never goes into the match record. A change of the setting reaches the rooms the person sits in at once (`Room.setNoAdapt`) and counts from their next match there.
* `AI_ADAPT` (wrangler var, default `off`): while off, an adaptive model plays its no evidence policy for everyone. With `on`, a v2 model reads the statistics only when its file carries a finite safety net (`meta.adapt`). The room never serves a v2 file without one: the model store refuses it and a fallback plays (see "Serving" below). Adaptation is switched on only after `project_docs/privacy-lia.md` section 5 describes the in-match reading. The model that ships today (v2, the twin, features `v2-empty`) reads no statistics at all, so the switch changes nothing for it ("What ships" below). For the candidate that passed the explorer test, the no evidence policy is weaker than its twin head to head (`ai.adaptation.ablation.offPolicy`), so once that candidate is adopted the reading is switched off for everyone by rolling back to the version that was current before it (`$C rollback v2` when no cycle promoted another), not with `AI_ADAPT` (`DEPLOY.md` section 9.5).
* When an AI plays for a person who dropped, the statistics stay with the seat, as the opponents see it.
* A person's profile from earlier matches is not read. `encodeStats` (`ai/src/opponent-stats.ts`) can take a prior per seat that replaces the population prior of that seat's in-match statistics, with the same weight, so a profile could one day become the starting point of the reading. The hook is off: `agentContext` never sets it, so the room, self-play and the benches all read the population prior, the only one the networks were trained with. The warm start check also runs with a prior per seat far from the population one, which changes the statistics inputs and nothing else. Switching it on needs training with simulated profiles first.

### Where the numbers are

Every measured number about adaptation is in `facts.json` under `ai.adaptation`, each with the date and the method: match length and evidence per decision, the priors, the warm start check, self-play speed, training runs, the regularizer and safety net sweeps, the gains per style against the twin and the ablation, head to head results against the panel, the explorer test, decision time, the characters on the adaptive model, the reading curve (how much the play changes hand by hand) and the checks for switching adaptation on (`switchOnCheck`). `history` keeps, per earlier candidate file, the keys that measured it. Each bench says at the top of its file (`ai/bench/`) what it plays and which key it writes. Every result file and facts key names the model files it measured with their SHA-256, and `ai/bench/adaptation-facts.ts` stops with an error when the keys about the served model measured different adaptive files.

## Serving

The rooms play models that the training cycle publishes to R2 (bucket `zap-models`, binding `MODELS`), with the models in the Worker bundle as the fallback: v2, the default, and v1 behind it (`ai/models/bootstrap/`, wired in `worker/src/cf/ai-runtime.ts`). The cycle itself (training, the promotion gate, the human test) is in `project_docs/cycle.md`; the formats are in `shared/cycle-formats.ts`.

### Files

* `manifest.json` (`zap-manifest/1`): the current version, the previous one while a test runs (`control`, `controlShare`, `controlUntil`), and one entry per version ever published: SHA-256, size, architecture, features, input and hidden sizes, safety net, and the persona knobs calibrated for that model.
* `models/vN.zapw`: the model file (int8). Never deleted, so a match pinned to an older version can always load it again.
* `cards/vN.json`: the model card, read by the cycle and the data page, not by the rooms.

### Loading (`worker/src/model-store.ts`)

* The manifest is read at most every 5 minutes per isolate, with one conditional get. A failed read, a body over 256 KB or an invalid manifest keeps the last good one; with none, the bundled v2 plays.
* A version loads from its manifest entry. A file whose SHA-256 equals one of the bundled files' is never read from R2: the bundled bytes are checked against the entry and played with the entry's knobs (`worker/src/ai-select.ts` for v1). Any other file is refused when it is missing, over 2 MB or not the entry's size, has another SHA-256, does not parse, has an unknown architecture or features version, disagrees with its entry (features, inputs, hidden sizes, safety net, version), or is a features v2 file without a finite safety net, and when the entry's persona knobs are invalid (each knob has a range: temperature from 0.05 to 10, lie and style shares from 0 to 1, offsets from -20 to 20, at most 8 persona inputs from -10 to 10; `KNOB_RANGES` in `shared/cycle-formats.ts`).
* A refusal is remembered per SHA-256 for the life of the isolate and logged once (`console.warn`), so a broken file is not fetched again on every match. Parsed files are kept per isolate, at most 4 besides the bundled one.
* Fallback for one version: its R2 file, then the bundled models in order (label `v2`, then `v1`: the first one whose file passes its own SHA-256 check), then the tuned heuristic (label `heuristic`). Before the first manifest every match plays the bundled v2. `AI_MODE=heuristic` plays the tuned heuristic in every seat.

### Versions per match (`worker/src/ai-select.ts`, `worker/src/room-core.ts`)

The room reads the manifest before each match start and fixes the versions for the whole match (`StoredMatch.ai`). A new manifest, a rollback or a wake from hibernation never changes them in the middle of a match: after a wake the room loads the pinned versions again before any move, and only a version that cannot be loaded is replaced, in the match and in its record.

| Situation | AI facing people | AI next to a person, and the AI playing for a person | Group (`arm`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No test | current | current | none |
| Test, quick play, with chance `controlShare` | previous | previous | `control` |
| Test, quick play, otherwise | current (the candidate) | previous | `candidate` |
| Test, room with friends | current | previous | none |
| Current version fails to load | fallback | fallback | none |
| Test, but the previous version fails to load | current (the candidate) | fallback | none |

A test runs while `manifest.control` is set, before `controlUntil`, and only when both versions loaded. When the previous version fails to load in an isolate during that time, the partners play the fallback instead of the candidate. So the candidate only ever sits in opponent seats, and a person's AI partner is never the candidate. The draw uses the cryptographic generator. The room shows the partners version (`PublicRoom.aiVersion`), the same in both groups, so a person cannot tell which group a match is in. The record (`zap-match/2`, `project_docs/data-dictionary.md`) keeps the version of every AI seat and the group.

Each version brings its own persona knobs from its manifest entry; an entry without knobs uses the characters' defaults (`ai/src/personas.ts`), except a bundled file, which keeps the knobs it ships with. Next to a person, an AI lies in at most 10% of its signals, whatever its knobs say. An AI that answers an action that is not legal (a broken model or knobs that slipped through) never stops a match: the room plays the base heuristic's move instead, else the first legal move (`worker/src/room-core.ts`).

### Local test sites

`.dev.vars` sets `TEST_RECORDS=true`, which marks every record `test: true`, and `MANIFEST_TTL_S=5`, which shortens the manifest cache (it counts only together with `TEST_RECORDS`). Neither is ever in `wrangler.jsonc`, and `worker/test/no-remote.test.ts` checks it. `worker/dev/local-r2.ts` opens the local buckets of the two local sites (and nothing else) to put a manifest and models there or to list the records they wrote.
