# The training cycle

How a new version of the Zap AI is made, tested and served, and how it is taken back. The code is in `training/cycle/` (`README.md` there lists the commands; `SERVER.md` covers the owner's server). How the AI itself works is in `ai.md`. Every number below is a setting of `training/cycle/config/prod.toml` unless it says otherwise.

## Versions

* A version is `vN`. v1 and v2 ship in the Worker bundle (`ai/models/bootstrap/`): v1 the first model, v2 the one that plays by default, gated against v1 with the production sizes before it was bundled (its card is `ai/models/bootstrap/card-v2.json`). `init` uploads both, v2 current. Every candidate that reaches the gate takes the next number, promoted or not, so a number never names two models.
* The models bucket holds `models/vN.zapw` (int8, at most 2 MB), `cards/vN.json` (the model card), `manifest.json` (which version plays) and `status/training.json`. A room reads the manifest at most every 5 minutes and checks size, SHA-256, architecture and features before it plays a file.
* The card of a version keeps what made it: the data counts and hashes (`recordsSha256` is the SHA-256 of one line `<key> <etag>` per record used), the training arguments, seed and league files, the commit, the full gate result, the characters' statistics, for a file that can read the in-match statistics its explorer test (`explorer`, below) and, after its window, the result with people.

## Training

* A candidate starts from the current version (its training checkpoint when the server has it, otherwise its weights) and keeps its features, hidden sizes and opponent priors.
* Training is self play with `training/train.py`: 390 iterations of 120,000 decisions, at most 10 hours. Its league holds every member of the gate panel: the random player, the base and tuned heuristics, v1 and the characters through the opponent mix, and the last 3 promoted versions as fixed league files for the whole run (`train.py --league-fixed`), after the window of the run's own last 8 snapshots. A model that never met an opponent in training is not judged against it.
* With `[gate] stats = "live"` (set when the owner switches the reading on) the opponent mix is the feints league of the network that passed the explorer test: `league_mix_live`, `feint_hands` and `feint_evidence` of `[train]`, the mix and switch points of `training/runs/m6-adaptive-r/args.json`, with 12% of the matches against opponents that fake a pattern and then switch. Later candidates keep that training, and the styles held out of it stay held out, so the fresh family of the explorer test stays outside training.
* The seed comes from the cycle id (the SHA-256 of `YYYYMMDD-HHMM`), so a cycle can be run again.

## The gate

Every game in the gate is a duplicate pair: the same deal twice with the seats swapped, so the luck of the cards cancels out. A pair scores 1, 0.5 or 0 for the candidate. The deals come from the cycle seed. The four segments are paulista and mineiro, 1 against 1 and 2 against 2. The AI plays with the in-match statistics at no evidence while adaptation is off in the rooms; with `[gate] stats = "live"` it plays them live, as the rooms will.

A candidate is promoted only when all four rules hold:

1. **Better than the current version.** A sequential probability ratio test (`sprt` of `ai/src/stats.ts`) on the pooled pair scores, with H0 a mean of 0.5 and H1 a mean of 0.515 (1.5 points better), alpha and beta 0.05. It looks after every round of 400 new pairs (100 per segment) and stops at the first accept or reject; at 40,000 pairs it stops as inconclusive, which does not promote.
2. **Not worse in any segment.** 2,500 new pairs per segment, one look. With `lo95 = mean - 0.5 - 1.645 se`, the segment passes when `lo95` is above -1.5 points.
3. **Not worse against the frozen panel.** Each member (random, base heuristic, tuned heuristic, v1 and the last 3 promoted versions) plays the candidate and the current version on the same deals, 2,500 pairs per segment. The paired difference is pooled over the segments with deals as clusters (`pooledPairedSummary` of `ai/src/evaluate.ts`); the member passes when `mean - 1.645 se` is above -1 point. With the standard errors the review measured on real models (at 1,000 pairs per segment: 0.33 points against random, 0.40 tuned, 0.47 base, 0.48 v1), a candidate exactly as strong as the current version against every member passes each one from 95% to 100% of the time at 2,500 pairs, and all of them, with two older versions, 81% of the time (1,000 pairs gave 15%); half a point better, 100% (a calculation made for the review of milestone 6). The extra pairs cost about 84,000 pairs per cycle, near 7 minutes on 4 threads at the measured 21 ms per pair; the twin comparisons use the same size.
4. **The characters keep their order.** The calibration on the candidate raises Otávio's temperature until he is the easiest on its own deals, so its order holds by construction. The gate measures the six characters again with the calibrated knobs on fresh deals from the cycle seed (`gate/personas-order.ts`, twice the calibration size per segment), and the rule fails only when the order is reversed beyond noise: Otávio's score against Zap minus the lowest of Bento, Lia and Ravi above 1.645 standard errors of that difference, or the highest of them minus Iara's above 1.645 standard errors. Both margins go into the gate result and the card.

Error rates. Each rule alone passes a candidate worse than its margin at most 5% of the time (one-sided 95% bounds; the SPRT has alpha 0.05). Since all rules must pass (an intersection union test), the chance to promote a candidate that is worse than a margin on any one rule stays at most 5% with no correction. The other side, how often an equal candidate is rejected, depends on the rules together; the cycle measures it once real candidates have gone through the gate after the long runs.

The gate result (`zap-gate/1`) lists every number and one plain sentence per failed rule. A rejected candidate leaves only its card.

### The explorer test

A file that can read the in-match statistics (features `v2` with a safety net in `meta.adapt`) is served reading once `AI_ADAPT` is on. An explorer is an opponent built to fake a pattern and then switch, so that the reading misjudges it; the adaptation part of what it gains is its score against the network minus its score against the same file with the statistics at no evidence, pair by pair. With `[gate] stats = "live"` and `[explorer] on`, every candidate that can read and passes the four rules goes through the explorer test before `publish` (`zapcycle/explorer.py`). It does against that one file what `_local/m6/run-feints.sh` did, with the sizes of that run in `[explorer]` (profile `prod` refuses smaller ones, `config.py` `EXPLORER_PROD_MIN`):

1. A scripted search ranked by the adaptation part over the original grid of explorers and the fresh family that no training league draws (`ai/bench/explorer.ts --stage search --baseline ablated --pool all`): successive halving in 4 rounds (`explorer.ts`), from 64 pooled pairs per candidate and player.
2. The chosen explorer on 2,000 fresh pairs per segment, with the best one from outside the training family when the chosen one is inside it.
3. Every explorer the cycle has on record, measured again against this file on 2,000 fresh pairs per segment: those that failed against an earlier file and those that passed alike, as `run-feints.sh` measured every earlier explorer again (`--remeasure` for a scripted one, `explorer-trained.ts --stage measure` for a trained one). The record lives in `state.json` (`explorerFailures`, with every file each explorer failed against, and `explorerMeasured`, with every file each was measured against) and in every card of a file that can read (`explorer.failuresOnRecord` and `explorer.measuredOnRecord`). It starts with every verdict of `facts.json` `ai.adaptation.explorer.criterion` (the scripted explorers with their specs; the trained explorers of `training/runs/m6-explorer-r` and `training/runs/m6-explorer` and the control of `training/runs/m6-explorer-twin`, with the files a later measure needs) and with the explorers `facts.json` records as failing (`ai.adaptation.explorer.criterion.history` and `ai.adaptation.history`). Every explorer a step measures joins it, the step's own PPO explorer with its files, and nothing leaves it.
4. A PPO explorer trained against this file as served (`training/train.py --league-as-served --episode match`, started from the bundled v2, the network without statistics, folded by `training/explorer_init.py`): 103 iterations of 120,000 decisions under a wall clock cap of 3 hours (`trained_hours`), then measured on 2,000 fresh pairs per segment. The step fails when the time left before the cycle's deadline cannot hold the whole cap, rather than train a shorter explorer, and it does not pass when the explorer stopped at the cap before its iterations (the card shows both in `explorer.ppo`).
5. The control explorer on record (trained the same way against the twin, `training/runs/m6-explorer-twin`) measured against this file next to the PPO explorer (`explorer-trained.ts --stage control`), as `run-feints.sh` did; its verdict counts like any other.
6. The criterion of `ai/bench/criterion.ts` (`explorer-trained.ts --stage facts`), read again by the cycle from the result files: every verdict's one-sided 95% upper bound of the adaptation part at most 1 point (`limit`, which the config may tighten and never loosen), and no explorer on record left without a verdict against this file.

Every seed comes from the cycle id, like the gate's, so a cycle can be run again and no two cycles share deals; inside a cycle each attempt plays deals no earlier attempt played. A candidate that fails is not promoted, and its card lists every verdict (explorer, kind, mean and upper bound of the adaptation part, pairs) and why. When a candidate will run the step, the training budget leaves it 6 hours (`reserve_hours`) of the cycle's 36. Since the record only grows, every cycle measures a few more explorers again: when the step no longer fits in its reserve it fails at the deadline (never with a shorter explorer), and `reserve_hours` is the setting to raise.

A failure is final for the network it was measured against. A candidate whose network (or file) is on record as failing is not measured again and never promoted, since measuring it again until one sample passes would defeat the bound by selection. Running the step again in the same cycle, adopting the same network under another version name, or a step stopped by a crash or the deadline all keep what was measured: each attempt has its own folder (`data/<profile>/explorer/<cycleId>/<attempt>/`) and the record only grows. Only the owner lifts such a failure, with `explorer --cycle ID --lift-failure "<reason>"` or `adopt EXPORT_DIR --lift-failure "<reason>"`: the network is then measured in full, with no verdict reused, and its card records the reason (`explorer.lifted`). With the statistics left `"empty"` or the step turned off while the version in play can read, the cycle stops before it trains, since its candidate could only be refused.

Verdicts are kept per network: a hash of the shapes, features, weights, safety net and priors of the file, which leaves out `meta.version` and the other metadata. A passing verdict for the same network is reused instead of steps 1 to 6 when every file it rests on is still on disk and still holds the same verdicts: the cycle's own (`data/<profile>/explorer/verdicts/<network>.json`) or the one of `facts.json` `ai.adaptation.explorer.criterion` (the served model file and the result files its verdicts name). The explorers recorded as failing after that verdict, or never measured by it, are still measured again, and their verdicts count with the reused ones. The adoption of the network that passed in milestone 6 therefore adds no explorer measurement when its files are on the server (`DEPLOY.md` section 9.2 lists them).

The guard: `publish` never promotes a file that can read without a passing verdict of this step for its network, whatever the config says: the step turned off, `[explorer]` missing, `[gate] stats` back to `"empty"` (the gate would have measured the file without its reading) or a forced pass in a local test all end in a refusal. It reads every verdict again from its result file, checks that the model files of a reused verdict hold the same network, and wants a verdict for every explorer on record, a PPO explorer that reached its iterations (read again from its `metrics.jsonl`), in profile `prod` the sizes of `run-feints.sh` among the `[explorer]` values the step ran with (the card keeps them in `explorer.settings`), and no failure on record against this network unless the owner lifted it for this step. A refusal writes a rejected card that says why and fails the cycle, so the training alert reaches the owner. A file that cannot read (features `v1`, `v2-empty`) is not concerned.

## The window with people

After a promotion the manifest names the new version as `current` and the previous one as `control` for 14 days:

* 30% of solo matches play the previous version everywhere (the control arm). In the other solo matches the new version sits only in opponent seats; a person's AI partner, and the AI that plays for a person who left, is always the previous version. In matches with more than one person the new version also sits only in opponent seats, and those matches belong to no arm.
* The record of each match says which version sat in each seat and which arm it was.
* Once a day the cycle counts the solo matches of each arm in the window, leaving out a seat that abandoned and every seat that may not go into a public number (no group because the person deleted their data, flagged, weight 0, out of open data). A match scores 1 when the AI team wins. The drop is the control arm's mean minus the candidate arm's: a positive drop means people beat the new version more often.
* The interval comes from a bootstrap over people (2,000 resamples, a person is the group of the latest daily export), from the 5th to the 95th percentile; that interval is what the card and `summary.json` show.
* The check looks every day for 14 days, so a single 95% bound would roll back a version exactly 2 points worse far more often than 5% of the time (the review measured from 12.5% to 18.5%). The rollback therefore reads a constant boundary near z 2.33, the 1st percentile of the bootstrap drops: when both arms have at least 200 matches and 20 distinct people and that boundary is above 2 points, the cycle rolls back on its own and the card says why. In the same simulation with daily looks (made for the review of milestone 6, 200 windows each), a version exactly 2 points worse is rolled back 3.0% of the time at 100 and at 300 solo matches a day and 6.0% at 1,000; equal versions from 0.5% to 1.0%; a version 5 points worse 17.5%, 50.5% and 88.5%.
* Matches alone are not enough: a bootstrap over people has almost no spread with one or a few people (with one person, none at all), so one person's matches would roll an equal version back on noise. The rollback therefore also needs 20 distinct people in each arm (`[ab] min_people_per_arm`); a person is a group of the latest export, and a match without a group counts as nobody. It is the same floor as the card's comparison below, and the config refuses any other value in profile `prod`, so the two cannot drift. A window that ends short of the people or the matches closes with the new version in place and says that the automatic rollback could not decide and why: in the status message, only until the next cycle starts (the scheduler starts one in the same tick); in `state.json` (`lastAbNote`, which `status` prints), until the next window ends; and in `training/logs/cycle.log`. Only `rollback vN` brings the previous version back then; if the cycle that started at the close already promoted a newer version, the rollback replaces that one too.
* `detectablePoints` is the true drop the rollback rule catches 80% of the time by the end of the window: `100 (0.02 + (2.326 + 0.8416) sd)` points, with sd the bootstrap's standard deviation.
* The card shows the drop and its interval only once both arms have at least 20 people; before that those numbers are null in the card and stay in the private status message and the run's report.
* At the end of the window the final numbers go into the card and the window closes. The scheduler runs that closing check as soon as the window ends, before it may start any cycle, and `run` and `publish` refuse while `state.json` still holds the window.
* No new version is promoted while a window is open or waiting for its close.

The owner can roll back at any time with `rollback vN`; it never waits for a running cycle.

## Human layers

The layers that learn from people are written and tested now and switch on by themselves once there is enough data (`training/cycle/zapcycle/human/`, Node tools in `training/cycle/node/human/`). Every cycle checks each threshold again after `build`. The thresholds are the `[thresholds]` table of the config; the settings of each method are the `[human]` table. A layer that fails is logged and reported off, and the cycle goes on: training never depends on a human layer. A person flagged by a cycle leaves every other layer of that same cycle at once.

A person here is a group token of the latest daily export: random, made again for every export, and known only to D1. The cycle never sees a pseudonym, a nickname or a device. Rows of two exports share their match ids, so the cycle could follow a person's seats from one export to the next, but never learns who the person is. When a cycle ends, whether it ended ok or failed, and after a `build` run on its own, the per person tables (`tables/` and `human/`) of every older work folder are deleted; only the newest folder keeps them (a failed cycle keeps its own, for a rerun). The daily A/B check keeps one more copy, `data/<profile>/ab/tables/`, rewritten every day with the newest export's tokens. Seats count for the layers below only when they are linked, have a weight above 0 and are not flagged; matches between friends do not count, and neither do automatic or forced moves.

| Layer | On when | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automation flags | every cycle with a daily export | four patterns per person, sent to D1 as `control/flags.json`, reviewed by the owner |
| Population priors | 100 people with at least 10 observations of a number | the starting point of "How Zap reads you", sent as `control/priors.json` every cycle |
| Player types | 200 people whose 8 numbers are all shown | a mixture of types, or "no distinct types" |
| Human behaviour model | 500 valid matches from at least 50 devices | a network that plays like people, in training only when it beats a baseline |
| Twin without human data | when the human model is in training | the same training without it, to measure what people's data adds |
| Open data | `OPEN_DATA_ENABLED=true` and 1,000 valid matches | the release package (zip); the first release by the owner's command, then one after every promotion |
| Adaptation to each player | not in this milestone | waits for features with profile inputs |
| Ranking | not in this milestone | the rating comes later |

### Automation flags

Over the matches that started in the last 14 days, a person is flagged for:

* `regular-times`: the spread of their decision times, (p90 - p10) / median, below 0.40 over at least 200 decisions. Times are measured on the server: a fixed delay of 700 ms behind 90 ms of network jitter reads about 0.26, a delay drawn from 0.6 to 0.9 s about 0.32, and no synthetic person went below 0.87;
* `around-the-clock`: matches started in at least 18 distinct UTC hours within one day;
* `volume`: at least 400 matches within 7 days;
* `identical-sequences`: at least 3 matches whose first 40 own decisions (action and 0.5 s time band) are identical, comparing only matches with at least 20 such decisions.

Only the person's own choices count: automatic moves are the AI's and forced moves are no choice. A person already flagged in the export is not checked again. Each flag carries the first reason found and its evidence in numbers and plain words (for example the time spread and how many decisions it covers), never a name. The flag reaches D1 through the reference `<export date>:<group>`; the owner reviews it with the `flags` tool (`training/cycle/SERVER.md`): `confirm` keeps it, `clear` lifts it. A flag that reaches D1 less than 15 days after the person's last clear is ignored, so the automation never undoes a review on evidence the owner already judged; the look back of 14 days must stay below those 15 (`config.py` refuses more, and `tests/test_config.py` reads both numbers). A flag for the same reason as the cleared one waits 90 days, so a person whose play keeps a pattern the owner judged harmless (much volume, say) is not flagged again every 15 to 21 days; another reason still counts from 15 days.

What these patterns find, in the synthetic population made for the review of milestone 6 (3 runs of 400 people with casual, regular, heavy and moving schedules): no person flagged; every bot with a fixed delay (with or without jitter) or a delay drawn from 0.6 to 0.9 s caught by `regular-times`; every bot that plays 24 hours a day caught by `around-the-clock` and `volume`. A program that waits a random time drawn over a wider range (from 1 to 3 s, or log-normal) and plays human hours, 12 hours a day, shows none of the four patterns and is not caught.

### Population priors

`node/human/profiles.ts` replays each record with `profileCounts` of `shared/profile.ts` (the same counting the room uses at the end of a match), sums the counts per person and per person and segment, and fits the priors with `fitPriors` (contract 1.12). A number comes from people only when at least 100 people (never fewer than 20, whatever the thresholds say) have 10 observations of it; otherwise it keeps the bundled self-play value. No person counts for more than 2% of a number's observations. The segments' values come first; the spread between people of the seven numbers that have segment values is then measured around each person's own mix of segments, because raise, run, accept, reraise, shown and cover differ about twice between 1 against 1 and 2 against 2, and bluff about three to four times (in v1 self-play, 200 matches per segment: 0.342 paulista 1 against 1, 0.093 paulista 2 against 2, 0.374 mineiro 1 against 1, 0.112 mineiro 2 against 2, the bundled `readingSegments`). The six in-match numbers live in `segments`, bluff in `readingSegments`; signal truth exists only at 2 against 2 and stays pooled. In the verification's check (400 synthetic people of one style, half playing 90% of their hands at 1 against 1 and half 90% at 2 against 2), bluff read 0.317 and 0.128 against one pooled reference of 0.222, and the spread between the two groups inflated its fitted strength to 16.8; with bluff per segment the two groups read 0.321 and 0.124 against references of 0.321 and 0.124, the fitted strength is 50 (the cap: no real spread between people), and the player types find one type (`.cache/verify-abcd-fix/segmix.txt` and `segmix_types.txt`). Each number records how many people it came from (`numbers[k].people`, null for a self-play value), and the reading names, number by number, whether its reference comes from people or from self-play. `shown` is a mean of card strengths, not a share of yes or no answers: one shown card counts as 0.25 / 0.07, about 3.6, answers (0.07 is the variance of one card in self-play, 0.0709 over 9,272 cards measured by `shared/make-default-priors.ts`). In a synthetic test of 1,000 people with a true strength of 20 (three seeds; the same case is a test in `worker/test/profile.test.ts`), the fit gave 19.3, 19.6 and 18.5, and the 80% interval of every reading covered the person's own value 79.7%, 77.8% and 78.5% of the time. A prior's strength stays from 2 to 50, so it is never worth more than 50 observations. People who asked "Don't adapt to me" are left out: no profile is kept for them, so none is computed here either. The file goes to `control/priors.json` every cycle; the layer reads as on once at least one number comes from people.

### Player types

On each person's reading numbers (posterior means under the fitted priors) as logit(value) - logit(population), the distance from the population value at the person's own mix of segments, for the people whose 8 numbers are all shown (raw values would sort people by the tables they choose): a Gaussian mixture with diagonal covariances, k from 1 to 6, chosen by BIC, each fit the best of 10 starts. Accept is left out of the clustering because it is 1 - run - reraise: run and accept move together (correlation about -0.95), and a diagonal mixture cut a population of one single style into 2 or 3 types along that line (the verification of milestone 6: 3 of 6 synthetic populations of 400 people with one style were published as 3 types). Without accept, the same 6 populations give one type each (`.cache/verify-abcd-fix/types_onestyle.txt`), and the three styles of the unit test still give three. A type's center is published with all 8 numbers, accept as 1 - run - reraise. The choice must hold up: over 50 bootstrap refits, made with the same 10 starts, the mean adjusted Rand index between the refit's types and the full fit's types must be above 0.7; otherwise the page says there are no distinct types. Types are published in `summary.json` only when each has at least 20 people.

### Human behaviour model

It predicts a person's next action from what that person sees.

* Model: the actor network of training itself (features v1, the hidden sizes of self-play), started from the current version's weights (a features v2 file gives its first 431 input columns) and fine tuned on people's decisions, with an L2 pull towards the start and early stopping on held out people. The self-play network is much more certain than people, so one temperature is fitted on the held out people and folded into the policy layer. Why this model: it starts from what self-play knows about truco, so a few hundred matches only have to move it towards people; it is an ordinary model file, so training plays it with no new code; it has 180,496 weights, and one epoch over 100,000 rows took 6.7 s on one CPU core (measured on 2026-11-22).
* Rows: every weighted decision (no friends, unlinked or flagged seats, no automatic or forced moves), in the network's canonical actions (cards by strength), capped at 300,000 rows by a stable sample.
* Split: 80% and 20% by a hash of the person's group token, so nobody is on both sides; 10% of the training people decide when to stop.
* Baseline: the frequency of each action per decision type and legal moves on the training people, with one added to every legal action.
* Test: accuracy and log loss per decision type (answer, onze, bet, card) for both, and a 95% interval over people of the model's log loss minus the baseline's, on bet decisions alone and on bet and answer decisions together. It passes when both whole intervals lie below 0. The interval is a Student t interval over the people's own mean differences (k - 1 degrees of freedom), whatever the number of test people. A percentile bootstrap over people passed a model no better than the baseline too often: about 5% of the time at 11 test people (the threshold of 50 devices), and from 2.75% to 8.0% at 30, 50 and 100 test people (the verification of milestone 6). With the t interval, one interval alone passes such a model from 2.35% to 3.10% of the time with 8 to 100 test people when people differ evenly, and from 2.2% to 4.95% when most people gain a little and a few lose a lot (2,000 draws per size, `.cache/verify-abcd-fix/hm_rule_fixed.txt`), against the nominal 2.5%; the rule needs both intervals below 0, so it passes such a model less often than one interval does. Only then is it saved in `data/<profile>/human/` and given to the candidate's training (`train.py --human-model`); the card records its SHA-256 and the result.
* In training it is its own opponent kind `human` (`ai/src/league.ts`): `[human.model] league_share` of the matches (8%), taken from the network playing itself, seat it as an opponent, and the partner swap of 2 against 2 also seats it as a partner. It plays with neutral knobs and no safety net, like the snapshots.
* As the seventh character on the site it still waits: the Worker would have to serve the human model (a file in `zap-models` and a persona id in the records, the room and the pages).

### The twin and the seed floor

When the human model is in training, the cycle trains a twin: the same start, the same seed, the same settings and the same number of iterations as the candidate really ran, with the same fixed league files and the plain league mix, so it never meets the human model. In the first cycle with a twin, and then every 4 cycles, a second twin with the seed base plus 1 measures how much two runs differ by seed alone. Both twins are compared as int8 files, like the candidate's export: candidate against twin and twin against second twin, in duplicate pairs over the four segments (`gate/compare.ts`). The card keeps both numbers, so a gain from people's data is read against the seed floor, not against nothing. A cycle without a second twin carries the latest floor into its card with the cycle that measured it (`seedFloorCycle`), and every card adds `seedNoise`: how far every floor measured so far sits from 0.5 (root mean square), the scale of seed noise once there are several. Nothing is decided from them.

### Open data

The package holds only matches whose every person is still linked, did not opt out and is not flagged (by the export or by this cycle). Each match gets a new random code, dates are ISO weeks and a person's decision time is a 0.5 s band. A cell (week, variant, players) is published only with at least 20 distinct people, and that floor holds whatever the thresholds say. No match id, seed, group token or room code goes in. Style figures: aggregates only, never one row per person. For each band of match counts (50 matches or more), the mean and the standard deviation of each reading number over the people of the band, when at least 20 people have it. The dictionary of the release is `training/cycle/opendata/DICTIONARY.md`; the license is CC BY 4.0. The package records the export it was built from. `open-data --upload` syncs first, refuses a package built from an older export or when the newest export is more than a day old (so a release reflects deletions and opt-outs up to that export, made at most a day earlier; a person who leaves after it is left out from the next release), then attaches the zip to a GitHub release with a token that reaches only this repository; the local profiles only print the requests. Every upload is recorded in `state.json` (`openDataReleases`).

Releases follow promoted versions, as the plan asks. The first release is always the owner's own command: `open-data`, a look inside the zip and the re-identification check of the assessment, then `open-data --upload`. After that, every promotion releases on its own while the layer is on: the cycle syncs, builds the tables, the readings and the package again when a newer export arrived since its own tables, and uploads with the same refusals (`cli.open_data_after_promotion`). Its answer goes into the run's result (`publish.openData`: `off`, `waiting` for the first release by hand, `empty`, `uploaded` or `failed`); a failure there never undoes the promotion. The switch stays off by default.

## What is public

`summary.json` in the public bucket feeds the data page. A number built from people's data appears only for a cell with at least 20 distinct people, counted over the seats that may go into open data (linked, not flagged, weight above 0, not out of open data); below that the cell is left out and a people count is null. The one exception is the total of matches people played against Zap and how many they won, which counts every quick play match. The state of each human layer is there too (`layers`: on or off, why, and its progress in matches and people), and the player types once each type has 20 people.

## Test data never reaches production

* Local sites mark every record `test: true`. A production sync that finds one fails the cycle; a local sync refuses records without the mark.
* The production config reaches only the R2 endpoint and the buckets `zap-events`, `zap-models` and `zap-public`, each with its own token. The local configs reach only the local S3 stand-in and buckets named `zap-test-`. Any mix is refused before a request leaves the machine.
* The local bridge between a local site and the stand-in reads and writes only this project's `.wrangler/` folders and the `zap-test-` buckets.
